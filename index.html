Coaching Rating

Coaching Rating is the overall quality rating of a Coach, based on multiple parameters and scored over a Review Cycle.

It is a rating of Red, Amber or Green, set by bands of an internal score on a scale of 0 to 10.

Definitions

Review Cycle

The most recent 6 scored classes with each student, across all students. It rolls — every scored class enters, and the oldest with that student drops out.

Coaching Quality Score (CQS)

The internal score for one class, 0 to 10. Instructional Delivery, never below the Professionalism base of 3 on a clean setup, less any Professionalism deductions. A flagged class scores 0 to 3.

Instructional Delivery (ID)

Five rubrics, each scored 0, 1 or 2 — Math Knowledge, Right Zone, High Ratio, Strong Motivation, Session Flow. Together they make 0 to 10.

Professionalism (B)

Sets the base of the score — 3.0 when nothing is flagged. Camera angle, Lighting, Video and Audio can only deduct from it. It never adds.

Flagged Class

Video or Audio in red. The class scores 0 to 3 — teaching sets the number, every other flag takes a little more off — enters the Review Cycle at that score, and the payout is clawed back per policy.

From score to rating

Coaching Quality Scores are averaged per student over the Review Cycle, then across students. The band gives the rating — Red 0 to <4 · Amber 4 to <7 · Green 7 to 10.

Important Notes

* Audio — adjusted for assessments and practice sheets

Mic-off time while an assessment or smart practice sheet is running is deducted before the bands apply — the student is working silently by design.

† A late student never costs the coach time

Minutes lost to a late student join are added back before the band is tested. Student timings are recorded as context, never scored.

‡ Use of pause option

One pause of up to five minutes is within allowance and never counts against Video — it is the right route for stepping away or muting. What gets flagged is the habit: pausing in most recent classes is raised in feedback, detected across classes, not within one.

Thresholds are internal — never shown to a coach

The note says what happened and what to do next class; the rating says where it landed. Publishing the bands would invite teaching to the number — and make any later re-cut feel like a moved goalpost.

The three verdict verbs

Every parameter lands as one of these — red, amber and green arrive as a verb, and the verb is what feeds the task list. Inside reports and tasks the verdict sits at the sub-rubric level — each check carries its own verb
Must Fix — Red

A parameter flagged red, or a rubric scored 0. The task is mandatory before the next class, and one Must Fix forces the class rating red.

Needs Work — Amber

A parameter flagged amber, or a rubric scored 1. Raises a task — the note, the snippet behind it, an acknowledgement.

All Good — Green

The parameter cleared — a rubric scored 2, nothing flagged. No task raised.

Policy triggers
  • Six repeats on the same parameter → policy trigger, raised automatically — whatever the score says.
  • Video and Audio red do not wait for six. The first one flags the class, caps it at 3 and claws back payout.
  • A policy trigger is not a score. Nothing here changes ID, B or CQS.
Green does not mean nothing to do

The ID score is a sum, so 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 0 = 7 reads green while raising two tasks — one Needs Work, one Must Fix. Every rubric below 2 raises its task regardless of the total; the number says where the class landed, the verbs say what to do next. A Must Fix forces the rating red, because a zero is exactly what a sum hides.

Four rules for the roll-up
  • The window is per student. Six classes each, rolling — a coach with four students carries up to 24 classes.
  • Under six, average what exists. Nothing is padded, and no student is excluded for being new.
  • Every student counts once. Per student first, then across students — one heavily booked student cannot decide the coach's number.
  • A flagged class is in the window. It enters at 0 to 3 — lower still if other flags sit on it — and drags the average. The policy trigger is raised beside it, automatically.

What evidence appears in the hero card, the weekly report, the class report and the task list — and how much of it, decided by the score.
Class reports carry the negative side

Negative instances appear in every class report, subject to scoring. Positive evidence appears only in the weekly report. What is shown, and how much, follows the rubric score below.

Every flag is backed

Every flagged parameter and sub-rubric carries negative evidence — the moment from the class — and a recommendation.

Q&A

Questions coaches ask about Coaching Quality — and the answers, in their language. No thresholds, no formulas.
What is a Review Cycle?

Your most recent 6 classes with each of your students, across all your students. The cycle rolls — every reviewed class enters, and the oldest with that student drops out.

How is my Coaching Rating decided?

Every class is analysed as per The Cuemath Way across your latest Review Cycle — the Instructional Delivery rubrics plus the Professionalism basics.

Why did my rating change when I only taught one class?

The cycle rolls — each new class enters and an older one leaves.

I teach one student far more than the others. Does that student decide my rating?

No. Six classes per student, so every student counts equally.

How do I get the top rating?

Follow The Cuemath Way and maintain the Professionalism basics, class after class.

Where do I see what to improve?

Actions arrive under Tasks, and every class has a detailed class report.

What am I expected to do with a task?

Read the note, watch the moment, acknowledge it — and do it differently next class.

What if I disagree with the feedback?

You can use the contest option on the task to add context, and your mentor will review it. Contesting never counts against you.

My student was working silently on an assessment. Will that silence count against me?

No. Time when an assessment or practice sheet is running is recognised and set aside — the student is meant to be working quietly then.

What exactly is The Cuemath Way?

Cuemath’s way of teaching — Strong Motivation, Right Zone and High Ratio — reviewed in every class alongside Math Knowledge and Session Flow.

I have fewer than 6 classes with a new student. How does that count?

The cycle uses what exists — no student is left out for being new.

How quickly can my rating improve?

As fast as you teach. Every strong class replaces an older one in the cycle, so the rating moves with your most recent work — in both directions.
